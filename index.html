      Your day · 2 of 3

      One small loop, every day.

      1. PlanPick one most important outcome, up to two others, and give them real time in your schedule.
      2. DoWork the plan. Check things off as they get done.
      3. Close the daySee what got done, and write down a memory and a lesson.
      4. DecideGive unfinished work a date, save it in Unresolved, or forget it. Until you decide, nothing gets lost.

      Your year · 3 of 3

      Keep the year in view.

      Horizon holds a Misogi — one hard thing that defines the year — six mini-adventures, three commitments and one winning habit per quarter, a rhythm we owe to Jesse Itzler. Every day you close paints a square of your year. It sits beside Day and Week: at the top of the planner, or in the bar at the bottom of your phone.

      1. Pick outcomes that matter

      Give one outcome visual priority, then keep only two other important outcomes. The hierarchy should remain obvious at a glance.

      2. Make every task verifiable

      Too vagueWork on the reportDraft the introduction and section 1 (max 3 pages)
      Too vagueTidy the deskDiscard old papers from the top drawer
      Too vagueEmailsProcess the inbox and reach Inbox Zero

      3. Put work on the schedule

      Click an empty slot to create a block. Click an existing block to edit it. Hold briefly and drag to move it; resize from the lower edge. The calendar action beside a task schedules that specific item. Start and End accept typed times or 15-minute menus, and a block can cross midnight for up to 24 hours.

      4. Categories and statistics

      Categories can represent projects or areas of life. Choose from three distinct color palettes, then archive a category when it is no longer active: its historical blocks keep their identity, but it disappears from new planning. Archived categories remain visible in Settings and can be restored. Day and Week statistics are derived from scheduled blocks.

      5. Choose the schedule range

      Settings lets you choose the hours you want visible and the days you normally plan. Week view shows those planning days side by side, each with its main outcome above its schedule; Close day uses them to find the next planning day. Inactive days are never blocked — you can still open or schedule them manually.

      6. Close the day deliberately

      Every unfinished item can go to a specific date, be Saved safely without a date in Unresolved, or be Forgotten — taken off your plate while its day keeps the history. The carried count and original date remain attached so repeated postponement stays visible.

      7. Keep the horizon visible

      Horizon is the planner’s third view, beside Day and Week: choose Day · Week · Horizon at the top left, or press Y. It keeps the year visible without turning it into another task manager, using a yearly rhythm inspired by Jesse Itzler: one Misogi period, three meaningful commitments, up to six Mini-adventures, quarterly Winning habits and significant Key dates grouped in the Year map. Winning habits are checked when you close the day; their activity stays available on demand. Choose Day or Week to come back to your plan.

      8. Find and change your view

      Search scans saved planner days. Press F outside a text field. Fit app to width enlarges only this planner — it never changes browser zoom for the rest of your domain.

      9. Floating mode & print

      Picture-in-Picture keeps a compact editable Plan / Schedule view while you work elsewhere. Print is optimized for a landscape planner sheet and hides editing controls.

      10. On your phone

      Plan, Schedule and Horizon sit at the bottom of the screen, and each says where you stand: how much of the plan is done, what is on now or next, and what comes next on your year. Horizon keeps the bar in view, so Plan or Schedule takes you straight back. The calendar button at the top left switches between Day and Week: in Week view, Plan lists your week day by day and Schedule shows the week grid — tap a day to open it. Search, Journal, Unresolved and Time stats are in the header; everything else is under More.

      11. Daily Planner Plus

      Plus is paid once and yours for life, every year: plan the year in a guided half hour, print it for your desk and fill it in by hand, then look back on it in Your year in review, on one printed page. Five more colours for your painted year and everything Plus gets later come with it. Open it from Settings or More → Daily Planner Plus. A gift link or a licence activates it too. It is checked on this device, without an account, and travels with your planner through sync and backups.

      12. Install it without creating an account

      Use your browser’s install action when it is available. On iPhone or iPad in Safari, use Share → Add to Home Screen. In Chromium browsers, look for Install app in the address bar or browser menu. The installed app uses the same local planner data and can work offline after the production app has been loaded once.
